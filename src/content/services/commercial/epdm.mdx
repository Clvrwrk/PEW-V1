EPDM roof repair keeps capital on the balance sheet when the rubber still performs. Pro Exteriors handles peel-and-stick patches, splice repairs, and curb rebuilds across [commercial roof repair](/commercial-roofing/repair/), [flat roof repair](/commercial-roofing/flat-roof-repair/), and full reinstalls when the assembly is exhausted. Rubber rewards disciplined seam chemistry: the wrong primer or splice tape on a twenty-year field creates a callback faster than a bad weld on TPO because water tracks under laps before interior stain appears.

## EPDM thickness guide

| Thickness | Typical service life | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 60-mil | 25–30 years | Pro Exteriors standard for general commercial |
| 80-mil | 28–35+ years | High traffic or hail exposure |

Thicker sheet buys puncture margin on logistics roofs with daily forklift paths and rooftop equipment service. Specifiers in hail corridors often pair 80-mil field with reinforced walkway pads rather than assuming membrane alone absorbs impact.

## Common EPDM failure modes

**Tape seams and field splices.** Factory tape that aged past its window, or splice primer applied in marginal weather, opens under heat cycling. Atlanta humidity accelerates adhesive fatigue at seams — a dominant driver on [commercial roofing Atlanta](/commercial-roofing/atlanta/) service routes.

**Flashings at penetrations and curbs.** Pipe boots, pitch pockets, and HVAC curbs account for more recurring leaks than open field sheet on mature EPDM. We rebuild curbs with compatible rubber and metal rather than smearing generic sealant.

**Shrinkage and membrane pull at terminations.** Older ballasted and mechanically attached systems show sheet pull at walls and parapets. Termination bars and adhesive-backed strip-ins need to respect manufacturer movement limits.

**Punctures and maintenance damage.** Unmarked walkways invite screwdriver drops and panel dents. Ballast displacement after storms exposes sheet to foot traffic. Hail bruising on rubber may not split the membrane immediately but compromises long-term elasticity — inspection after significant events is standard on DFW portfolios where NOAA data records **1,609** hail events (core DFW counties, 2016–2026, max **5.90 in** reported) — *Source: hail_by_metro.md / NOAA Storm Events via Supabase `zcta_hail_summary`, read 2026-11-19.* Atlanta’s metro record is sparser (**97** events, max **2.50 in** in the same window) — same source — but wind-driven debris still punctures aging rubber.

**Wet insulation under sound-looking rubber.** Infrared before major EPDM roof repair is non-negotiable. Patching over saturated polyiso throws money at a vapor trap. When moisture spread exceeds economical patch boundaries, we document replacement economics on the [commercial roof repair](/commercial-roofing/repair/) pillar.

## Clean edges, terminations, and repair discipline

EPDM roof repair fails when edges are dirty, primers are applied in marginal weather, or termination bars are short. Clean substrate and manufacturer splice sequence matter more than extra caulk. Document repaired areas with photos at curbs, parapets, and pipe boots so facilities can audit workmanship on the next maintenance cycle.

## EPDM roof repair on low-slope roofs

Most **low-sloped roofs** on medical offices, schools, and retail pads still carry **60-mil EPDM** or ballasted rubber from recover cycles. **EPDM roof repair** in our queue is rarely a store-bought **EPDM repair kit** slapped over a drip: cured cover tape, splice primer, and compatible **roofing material** are billed as matched assemblies. **Seam leaks** usually trace to open laps at curbs or factory seams under RTU curbs; **punctures** and **tears** near walkway pads show up as circular stains on core cuts.

| Symptom | Typical scoped approach |
| --- | --- |
| Edge drip at termination bar | Strip-in, new bar, clean **edges**, mechanical attachment |
| Field seam open | **EPDM patch** or cover tape per manufacturer sequence |
| Curb intrusion | Membrane section + curb flashing rebuild |
| Ballast-saturated field | Ballast pull-back, insulation replacement, lap rebuild |

**Rubber membrane** sections on large recover jobs pull insulation replacement into the **$4,050** national repair-proposal median (IQR **$2,035–$7,879**), not the **$902.50** leak-call median — plan budget tiers separately before the board sees a single line item.

## Roof membrane repairs owners should expect

**Roof repairs** on **EPDM rubber** should name wet insulation zones, adhesion test results, and whether **rubber patch** work is interim or warranty-grade. **Roof membrane** documentation belongs in the closeout packet: primer type, tape batch, and areas excluded from warranty because of ponding or foot traffic. Where **commercial roofing** portfolios mix EPDM with TPO additions, keep system labels on tickets so the next vendor does not weld over rubber laps.

Facilities teams managing humid climates should pair **EPDM roof repair** with scheduled seam walks — Atlanta portfolio data shows multiple leak points per building when curbs and terminations lag maintenance cycles.

## Repair, coat, or replace on rubber

Use scoped repair medians when seams and flashings are localized. Consider commercial roof coating after adhesion tests and seam rebuild when the field is oxidized but dry. Move to recover or replacement when infrared maps wet insulation across zones or when repeat leak tickets hit the same building within twelve months.
